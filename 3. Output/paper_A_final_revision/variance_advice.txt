VARIANCE DECOMPOSITION: ACTIONABLE MODEL AND DIAGNOSTIC PLAN
24 October 2026. Read-only advisory review; no models fitted and no canonical code edited.

I read and acknowledge run_constraints.md. This advice concerns corrected B with the example-specific screen and same-six-attribute pooled reference. B retains shared fields under the stated invariant-choice assumption; C and no-screen are required sensitivities. It does not alter the attribute wording, author edits, raw data, or paper B.

Evidence reviewed
The prior proposal/reconstruction review; reconstruct.R; point_start_audit.R; optimizer_audit.R; derivative_flag_audit.R; bootstrap_v3.R; B point/covariance/bootstrap diagnostics; and numerical-audit CSVs. These outputs precede the present screen/reference correction and cannot supply final numbers.

Old B was not simply an optimizer failure: 2,157 complete-covariate teachers, 17,256 decisions, 34/34 fixed-effect rank, 21 covariance parameters, final BOBYQA gradient about 0.0001, Hessian minimum 75.13, covariance minimum eigenvalue about 7e-16. An independent nloptwrap fit had log likelihood within 4.4e-5 and allocation differences below 1.6e-5, although its near-zero eigenvalue crossed the isSingular threshold. Thus convergence, near-boundary covariance and practical stability are different questions. Its 148 singular fits among 199 bootstrap replicates cannot establish six independently supported random directions. The old gradient warning in the point JSON belonged to a previous optimizer attempt. Preserve attempt-specific logs in the new run.

1. Freeze the estimand before model comparison
Use exactly the same corrected-B observations, response-derived harshness, fixed effects, weights, attribute coding and centering in every candidate. Retain the six student main effects, 15 pairwise student interactions, professional measures, beliefs and observed harshness fixed block; no random intercept. Retain all six numeric random slopes. Record why this decomposition is descriptive: harshness is constructed from the same responses, and the linear Gaussian covariance model is a working representation of binary-response variation. It is not an identified psychological mechanism, an independent teacher trait, an external predictive R-squared or a causal accounting.

Choose centering once before fits, e.g. corrected-B complete-case decision-weighted attribute means, then freeze those constants for all three covariance candidates, optimizers, and paired sensitivities if the goal is a common anchor. Record the constants. With no random intercept, changing the centering changes the random-curve covariance model, not merely the intercept label. Do not choose the center because it improves fit or shares. If a full-estimator bootstrap instead recomputes centers, explicitly declare that rule and use it in every replicate; do not mix conventions. Keeping the original-B anchor fixed is the simpler common-model comparison.

Construct the pooled profile key from all six binary attributes. Verify no teacher has two observations of the same pooled profile. Harshness must exclude the teacher's own contribution to each profile reference; if duplicates exist within teacher, subtract all of that teacher's observations of that profile. This is stronger than subtracting only the current row. Keep all eligible teachers in the reference population before restricting the decomposition to complete X3 cases; do not accidentally change the reference by doing listwise deletion first.

2. Check design support separately from covariance identification
Tabulate decisions per teacher, within-teacher variation for each attribute, the distribution of rank(Z_i), and variation/rank by assigned set. Retain incomplete teachers under the stated sample rule; lack of both levels in a particular teacher is not grounds to delete that teacher if variance components are identified across teachers.

Check full fixed-effect matrix rank, dropped columns and condition number. A full-rank fixed-effect design, 56 supported profiles and many teachers do not themselves identify all random covariance entries. The relevant marginal covariance is V_i = Z_i G Z_i' + sigma^2 I. For each candidate, stack the lower triangles of its covariance-basis matrices across the observed teacher/set designs, including I for residual variance, and check the resulting linear map's rank and scaled singular values. Full G requires 21 independent covariance directions plus the residual direction; block G requires 9 plus 1; diagonal G requires 6 plus 1. Account for actual observed/missing patterns. This is a structural support check, not a fitted-eigenvalue test. A full-rank but ill-conditioned map can still imply weak practical identification; report conditioning rather than equating rank with precision. Near-flat restricted-likelihood curvature provides complementary practical evidence.

3. Lock a limited candidate set, not a search over attractive allocations
The coordinator's three candidates are appropriate:
F: all six correlated slopes; one 6x6 covariance matrix, 21 parameters.
K: three independent 2x2 blocks: failed/competence, absent/disruptive, male/background; 9 covariance parameters.
D: six independent slopes; 6 variances, zero covariances.
All three retain six attribute-specific variances. K allows correlation inside academic, engagement and sociodemographic domains, while imposing zero cross-domain covariance. D imposes all 15 covariances to zero. Those restrictions are assumptions and are basis-dependent. They are not conclusions that correlations do not exist. K's grouping is substantive rather than chosen from the largest fitted correlations. This is a rule fixed at this revision stage, not a preregistered model choice.

Suggested deterministic selection rule to record BEFORE inspecting new allocations:
(a) Exclude from primary-model consideration candidates that fail structural identification or unresolved numerical checks below. Show the failure in the diagnostic table.
(b) Among numerically stable, nonboundary candidates, minimize REML-AIC; for AIC differences of 2 or less, choose the candidate with fewer covariance parameters. Identical fixed-effects design and identical response rows are essential for this REML comparison. Thus K must improve AIC by more than 2 over D to displace D, and F must improve by more than 2 over the best simpler admissible candidate. Record all objectives and AIC values, not just the winner. The threshold is a pragmatic parsimony rule, not a hypothesis test.
(c) A singular F is a valid boundary estimate and remains a sensitivity, but should not be the primary basis for claims about a full six-dimensional covariance distribution. A singular K cannot establish that all three correlated pairs are supported. Prefer a stable nonboundary simpler candidate if available under this declared rule.
(d) If D has one or more zero slope variances but is numerically stable, do not delete teachers or search slope subsets until nonsingularity appears. Keep those zero estimates, label the effective dimension and the boundary. Such an aggregate descriptive allocation may still be useful if it is stable. If all candidates are boundary models, do not pretend that the above interior-AIC rule selected a securely identified covariance structure; state the fallback to D explicitly and report the full sensitivity range. If even D is numerically unresolved or the slope/residual split is materially sensitive, move the decomposition to the appendix rather than continue specification hunting.

AIC here is only supporting working-model comparison: observed harshness depends on y, so the fitted likelihood is not a complete generative model for the joint construction. AIC improvement, optimizer agreement and nonzero eigenvalues do not prove omitted cross-domain covariances are zero. Report F/K/D allocations side by side regardless of selection. Predeclare an absolute change exceeding 2 percentage points in any allocation as a material sensitivity to discuss; this threshold is for presentation/interpretation and MUST NOT choose the model. Also report the combined random-slope plus residual share, since it may be more stable than their split.

Reduced-rank alternatives should not expand this run's candidate search. rePCA is useful to describe the full fit's estimated dimensions; rotating into eigenvectors estimated from the same outcomes and then calling the reduced structure prespecified is incorrect. A formal factor-analytic model needs a declared rank/loading identification and uncertainty for estimating the rotation. Zero estimated eigenvalues of F already reveal a fitted reduced rank, but do not identify which named attribute slope should be deleted. Regularized Bayesian models would add prior-dependent assumptions and are not a quick numerical cure.

4. Numerical audit for point models
Use REML, full derivative calculations and stricter fixed tolerances. For each candidate run nloptwrap with adequate evaluations and ftol_abs/xtol_abs around 1e-8; independently run BOBYQA with comparable precision. Include automatic initialization and restart from the attained optimum; use deterministic small perturbations if warnings persist. Do not select the first optimizer giving the desired shares. Select the best attained objective among verified solutions, retaining all attempt logs. If two optimizers disagree materially, use allFit or a third optimizer for adjudication.

Record optimizer code, finite objective, warnings, raw/scaled gradient, Hessian eigenvalues and condition number, covariance eigenvalues relative to sigma^2, isSingular(tol=1e-4), rePCA SDs/rotations, residual variance, and fixed-design rank. Inspect nearby singularity tolerances descriptively; do not tune the threshold to relabel a near-boundary solution.

Use gradient <0.002 and positive Hessian as routine interior checks. Recompute questionable gradients/Hessians by Richardson extrapolation on the same objective. At constrained boundaries, do not insist on an unconstrained zero gradient or reject solely for a failed Cholesky scaling: assess feasible-direction/KKT behavior and whether perturbations/restarts reduce the objective. Nor does a positive Hessian prove that G has full rank. Keep singularity and numerical-failure flags separate.

Prespecify practical optimizer agreement, e.g. absolute difference in optimized REML criterion <=1e-3 and maximum allocation difference <=1e-4 (0.01 percentage points), then report actual differences. A nearly equal objective with materially different covariance entries indicates weak component identification even when aggregate allocations agree. Flat-curvature or optimizer differences must be resolved/documented, not hidden by rounded percentages. Report conditional predictions outside [0,1] as a limitation of the working LMM; do not use clipping to modify allocation.

5. General allocation formula and implementation trap
Let eta = X beta be the population-level predictor; let phi_k be the existing Shapley allocations to the four fixed blocks. Verify sum(phi_k) = sample Var(eta), using the same variance convention and rows throughout. For independent random-effect blocks b with designs Z_b and covariance G_b, set
V_random = (1/N) sum_r sum_b z_rb' G_b z_rb.
For six diagonal slopes this becomes
V_random = sum_j tau_j^2 * mean_r[(x_rj - c_j)^2].
For K add the within-block covariance cross-products; for F use the full quadratic form. Do not average the variance of BLUP predictions: shrinkage makes that a different quantity. Use actual-scale VarCorr covariances (which already include sigma^2), not unscaled theta unless explicitly converted.

Normalize every phi_k, V_random and sigma^2 by T = Var(eta) + V_random + sigma^2. This produces the same descriptive accounting convention as the existing code; it is not an exact sample ANOVA identity for y or independent causal sources.

CRITICAL: the existing allocation extracts only as.matrix(VarCorr(m)$id). In D or K lme4 creates multiple covariance blocks for the same id grouping factor (possibly named id, id.1, etc.). Extract and sum ALL relevant blocks with correctly matched design columns; otherwise five slopes or two entire domains can silently disappear. The explicit six (0 + x_c | id) terms are the clearest D syntax; numeric (0 + all_six || id) is also supported. Assert exactly six total random-effect columns and the intended 6/9/21 theta count. Compare the generalized result against explicit diagonal and full quadratic forms on identical fitted values. Every bootstrap must use the same generalized allocation.

6. Bootstrap uncertainty
Use a teacher/ResponseId cluster nonparametric bootstrap, resampling all that record's retained decisions and covariates together. Rebuild pooled-profile leave-teacher-out references and harshness in every replicate, assign sampled copies unique clone IDs, then apply complete-covariate restrictions and refit the chosen fixed covariance specification. Include the broader reference population, not only complete-X3 teachers. If there is one row per profile per teacher, subtracting the own clone's one row is the correct shortcut; duplicated original teachers in the resample are independent sampled clones. Use all retained decisions for each clone, including incomplete records. Check every denominator and finite harshness. Freeze the chosen model across replicates; rerunning model selection would estimate a different selection-inclusive procedure.

Use paired teacher draws across F/K/D for uncertainty in model differences if that comparison is bootstrapped, and paired draws across B/C when interpreting their differences. For fixed design-mix inference, stratified resampling by assigned set/context is an option, but declare it and retain the same target across analyses; ordinary cluster resampling targets the empirical mixture. Do not change the resampling scheme after looking at widths.

Use 199 replicates only as a smoke test or explicitly coarse exploratory uncertainty. About 999 or more completed replicates is preferable for final percentile endpoints; preserve deterministic seeds, fingerprints and checkpointed draw results. Calculate full derivatives for D/K replicates if feasible. For each draw use the same deterministic fallback optimizer rule on failures/warnings and retain the original draw after successful numerical rescue. Do not redraw until a fit is nonsingular. Boundary fits that are numerically valid belong in the sampling distribution. Log all failures, boundary frequency, gradients, objectives and fallback attempts; optimizer code 0 alone is not the final acceptance criterion. If full derivatives are omitted for expensive F sensitivity replicates, perform a declared fixed-index and all-flagged numerical audit and explicitly label its weaker checking.

Do not silently discard failed replicates and label the remaining percentiles a 95% confidence interval. Report failures and remedial attempts; substantial unresolved failure means uncertainty is not ready. A high success proportion such as 95% is an operational alarm threshold, not a theorem validating conditional intervals. Report Monte Carlo precision/coarseness. Variance estimates on a boundary can have point mass at zero; ordinary percentile cluster-bootstrap ranges are descriptive estimator spread and need not have nominal coverage there. Avoid Wald symmetric intervals for variance/correlation parameters. A standard bootMer call conditions on the supplied harshness covariate and therefore misses its reconstruction; a Gaussian parametric LMM bootstrap also simulates nonbinary responses and does not solve that problem. It is not the preferred main uncertainty calculation for this outcome-derived decomposition.

Recommended stopping point
Fit only F/K/D on corrected B; apply the declared identification/numerical/parsimony rule; bootstrap the selected stable specification with the entire reference/harshness recipe; fit the same selected structure in C and no-screen; report all three B covariance sensitivities and required sample sensitivities. Keep the figure in the body only if the aggregate interpretation survives them. No more candidate mining is necessary.

Primary methodological sources checked online
The lme4 documentation explains that singularity concerns zero-variance linear combinations, can occur without a zero named variance or +/-1 pairwise correlation, and is distinct from numerical convergence: https://lme4.github.io/lme4/reference/isSingular.html .
The lme4 convergence guide supports tighter tolerances, restarts, Richardson derivatives and optimizer comparisons: https://lme4.github.io/lme4/reference/convergence.html .
The lmer reference documents independent numeric slopes and theta/Cholesky parameterization: https://lme4.github.io/lme4/reference/lmer.html .
The rePCA documentation describes covariance-dimension diagnostics: https://lme4.github.io/lme4/reference/rePCA.html .
The bootMer documentation distinguishes simulation of new random effects from conditioning on fitted effects and provides warning/failure reporting: https://lme4.github.io/lme4/reference/bootMer.html .
Bates, Kliegl, Vasishth and Baayen, Parsimonious Mixed Models (2018 version), motivates dimension/complexity diagnostics: https://arxiv.org/html/1506.04967v2 . Its hypothesis-testing simplification workflow is not a mandate to select the decomposition by significance. The limited candidate set, AIC tie rule, material-sensitivity tolerance and bootstrap recommendation above are my study-specific advice, not direct prescriptions from those sources.
