Result and editorial recommendation
1. Reuse the existing tercile-slope table, with a clearer introduction connecting overall harshness to the different profile attributes associated with recommendations. This is the strongest immediately useful presentation change. Say "descriptive differences in attribute associations across observed harshness groups" or "patterns in the application of criteria." The groups are defined by those same outcomes, so neither the coefficients nor their differences independently identify latent sensitivity variation or three teacher types. Teacher-clustered SE describe the reported regressions; they do not remove selection into response-defined groups. Do not assert between-group statistical differences merely from separate within-group significance stars.

The two academic attributes provide a clear concrete illustration. The current canonical table reports failed-subject coefficients 31.57, 65.05 and 66.95 percentage points, versus competence coefficients 27.16, 28.56 and 25.94. The extracted full-eight sensitivity reports 31.93, 66.08 and 66.28 versus 27.33, 28.05 and 26.58. The substantive descriptive illustration is similar, but these are different tables: canonical B has 2,604 teachers with usable responses (the within-teacher estimates omit singletons); B_full8 has 2,520 teachers. Reusing the existing table does not authorize silently replacing it with the full-eight coefficients. Full-eight tercile coefficients also cover more teachers than the 2,164 complete-covariate teachers in the mixed model.

2. Recommend a compact main-text table with six rows and two columns (attribute; primary model-implied random-slope SD in percentage points), subject to author approval. Its value is to make the six sensitivities concrete; readers currently see their joint 18.80% allocation but cannot see their relative fitted dispersion. Put the diagonal / three-pair / unrestricted comparison in Appendix C, alongside the existing covariance audit. A three-specification main-text table is defensible if the author wants model sensitivity immediately visible, but it would give boundary models disproportionate prominence in the narrative. The preferred split makes the substantive quantity readable while retaining transparent specification dependence nearby.

The B_full8 diagonal estimates round to 26.99, 25.67, 16.14, 10.55, 8.36 and 4.55 in that order. The final proposed main column must match the primary sample and model ultimately approved.

The table note must identify these as SDs of the fitted latent teacher-specific slope deviations, conditional on the model's fixed part, including response-derived harshness. With binary attributes, multiplying sqrt(variance) by 100 gives percentage-point dispersion in the attribute sensitivity; centering changes neither the 0-to-1 attribute contrast nor that unit. They are not raw SDs of observed teacher estimates, standard errors, uncertainty intervals, estimates of a share of decision variance, or direct dispersion of preferences. A one-SD value is not itself an observed response of a particular teacher. The joint sensitivity allocation also depends on the profile design and covariance terms; do not turn these six SDs into six additive variance shares.

No per-SD uncertainty is available in the reviewed outputs. The 199 accepted bootstrap draws save aggregate allocation shares and numerical diagnostics, not each slope variance or covariance. Their share SE cannot be assigned to the proposed SDs, and uncertainty cannot be recovered from these columns alone. A descriptive point-estimate table with "uncertainty not estimated here" is reviewable without new fitting; inferential comparisons, precise ranking claims or claims of well-estimated individual variance components would require additional authorized uncertainty work. Specification/sample ranges can be shown as sensitivity ranges, never confidence intervals. Keep covariance-structure comparisons distinct from comparisons that also change the analyzed sample or harshness reference.

3. Do not promote the correlations to a substantive psychological or preference-substitution finding. Keep them, if desired, as a short numerical illustration in Appendix C explaining why the diagonal covariance assumption is a restriction and why richer specifications require caution. Failed-subject / competence correlations are -0.625 in the three-pair model and -0.616 unrestricted; absence / discipline correlations are approximately -1.000 and -0.922. At most these are fitted associations among latent slope deviations in this conditional working model. They do not show that teachers exchange one actual preference for another, that increasing one sensitivity reduces another, or that two stable types exist.

Conditioning on harshness formed from the same eight binary decisions matters specifically here: it constrains the remaining patterns being modeled and can create or alter dependence among residual sensitivities. The absence of a separate teacher random intercept and the limited repeated-response design further delimit interpretation. Similar academic correlations in the two richer fits do not overcome that conditioning, the boundary solutions or the lack of correlation uncertainty. No empirical correlation of BLUPs is needed or suitable as a population covariance estimate.

Do not say the richer specifications fail to converge or have no support: they attain substantially better same-data REML AIC (5787.63 diagonal; 5066.51 pairs; 4990.23 unrestricted). Their optimizer diagnostics and objectives agree closely. Nevertheless, the pair fit has an essentially zero covariance eigenvalue and the unrestricted fit is singular, so interior, independently supported covariance directions cannot be assumed. The pairs singularity flag changes with optimizer/tolerance while its near-boundary character remains: bobyqa isSingular=TRUE, nloptwrap=FALSE with minimum block eigenvalue around 4.55e-10. Report this as a boundary issue rather than choosing the favorable flag. Near invariance of the aggregate allocation is evidence about that allocation's sensitivity, not proof that the diagonal restriction is empirically correct or that individual covariance estimates are substantively reliable. Zeros imposed outside the pair blocks, as well as all diagonal-model covariances, must be labeled restrictions; the extraction CSV's zero entries are not estimated null findings.
