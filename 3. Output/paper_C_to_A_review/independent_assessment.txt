JUDGEMENT: WHAT PREDICTION ADDS
The held-out outcome has not contributed to its own H, and the external teachers' evaluation cards have not trained references or calibration parameters. This addresses the mechanical same-response objection to using full-response harshness in A's decomposition. It asks a different question: whether information in four observed answers helps predict four masked answers of the same teacher in the same encounter, after learning the pipeline on other teachers. Training teachers' target answers legitimately estimate calibration. Other training outcomes entering shared profile references do not contaminate the external teacher's targets. Random masking is not chronological forecasting; the ordered sensitivity remains within one encounter.

The observed gain provides useful evidence of repeatable within-encounter response information rather than merely an in-sample fit improvement. It does not validate the decomposition's percentages, identify its random-slope components, or prove that the signal is an enduring personal trait. Random intercepts or other shared response influences would already make gains plausible: the gain establishes their predictive usefulness under this procedure, not a new mechanism. The sizable relative reduction in Brier loss is worth one compact result, but is insufficient grounds by itself for rebuilding A around prediction.

BASELINE AND CLAIM
The baseline is stronger than an additive six-attribute regression: p_x is the training frequency for the complete six-attribute profile, with smoothing, so profile main effects and profile interactions are represented nonparametrically. Its logit coefficient is fixed at one, with an estimated intercept and additive Treatment, set and stage terms. This is a reasonable transparent benchmark for the exact claim 'incremental over full-profile frequencies and these additive context controls'. A free calibration slope or a suitably regularized alternative would be useful benchmark sensitivity if the predictive result becomes a headline; it is not a discovered implementation failure.

Additive context terms do not represent context-by-profile response differences. History can proxy such differences, as well as general severity, response styles or common encounter influences. Random assignment does not remove these possibilities within a teacher. A stronger claim about teacher-specific information beyond fully observed context would require a fair, train-only, appropriately regularized interaction benchmark and the same held-out evaluation. Do not demand a sparse saturated interaction model merely to manufacture an uninformative comparator; the strength of the benchmark should match the claim. With current outputs, retain the narrower claim and explicit limitation.

The current X contains Treatment, set and stage only. It does not establish gains beyond the professional/demographic and belief variables discussed in A, nor show that history outperforms those variables. Juxtaposing a 12.5% Brier reduction with less than 5% in-sample harshness R-squared would compare different outcomes, samples and metrics. A substantive ranking requires a matched predictive ladder (profile/context; plus measured covariates; plus history), on the same 2,164 eligible teachers, folds, history/evaluation partitions and losses, with honest handling of unavailable covariates. Beliefs were collected after recommendations, so such a comparison concerns association under available survey information, not a deployable prior-information predictor. No such new exercise is needed if A avoids that ranking and retains its covariate regressions as a separate descriptive question.

JUDGEMENT: WHAT STANDARDIZATION ADDS
Equalizing the other four attributes removes a real compositional ambiguity in the existing four-category comparison and clarifies its target population of profiles. It does not equalize set, treatment, stage or the complete card history. Its scientific contribution is a cleaner presentation/robustness of an existing finding, not an additional mechanism or unrestricted attribute effect. The restricted support and uniform weights define a different estimand from frequency-weighted observed-category summaries, so a replacement must be labeled and explained.

Within a binary profile, disagreement is deterministically 2*n/(n-1)*p*(1-p). It is not a second independent evidentiary discovery in addition to that profile's recommendation rate. Averaging within profiles is nevertheless informative: it distinguishes genuine division on identical profiles from a category mean near one half produced by consensus in opposite directions across its component profiles. Keep that explanation; do not count rates, disagreement, and their related contrasts as independent corroborations. If three simultaneous contrast bands are retained, report the defined family without treating it as a broad search for new interactions.
