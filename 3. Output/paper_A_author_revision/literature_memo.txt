PROPOSED ENGLISH TEXT (under 100 words; existing keys)
Studies with stronger controls for pre-retention differences produce less negative achievement estimates, while initial advantages over younger same-grade peers diminish with time \citep{allen}. A later meta-analysis finds an average effect around zero, with more favorable results in the short run and in same-grade comparisons, and less favorable results against same-age promoted peers \citep{goos}. Reviewing studies that apply causal-inference methods, \citet{valbuena} conclude that potential benefits are generally weak relative to retention's costs, with effects depending on policy timing and institutional setting.

1. Allen, C. S., Chen, Q., Willson, V. L., & Hughes, J. N. (2009). Quality of research design moderates effects of grade retention on achievement: A meta-analytic, multilevel analysis. Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis, 31(4), 480–499. DOI: 10.3102/0162373709352239. Existing key: allen.
Publisher: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0162373709352239
Author manuscript: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2921809/
Exact locators: Abstract; Results, Effect Size-Level Analyses / Median year post retention; Discussion. The abstract reports 207 effects from 22 studies, less negative estimates under better designs, and declining effects with years after retention. The manuscript's Discussion interprets the same-grade pattern as an initial boost that dissipates; decline per additional year is 0.11 for grade comparisons versus 0.04 for age comparisons. This supports the first sentence without treating all retention estimates as negative.

2. Goos, M., Pipa, J., & Peixoto, F. (2021). Effectiveness of grade retention: A systematic review and meta-analysis. Educational Research Review, 34, Article 100401. DOI: 10.1016/j.edurev.2021.100401. Existing key: goos.
Publisher: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1747938X21000245
Exact locators: Abstract; Introduction, paragraph beginning Fourth, previous meta-analyses. The abstract reports 84 methodologically sound studies, an overall average zero effect, more favorable short-run and younger-grade-mate comparisons, and less favorable same-age comparisons. The Introduction explains that grade comparisons assess development after an extra educational year against younger students, whereas age comparisons follow promoted contemporaries. The overall mean spans developmental outcomes; retain the broad wording average effect rather than relabeling it an achievement-only estimate. Comparison approach changes the question answered, alongside time since retention.

3. Valbuena, J., Mediavilla, M., Choi, Á., & Gil, M. (2021). Effects of grade retention policies: A literature review of empirical studies applying causal inference. Journal of Economic Surveys, 35(2), 408–451. DOI: 10.1111/joes.12406. Existing key: valbuena.
